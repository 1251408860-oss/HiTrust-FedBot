To the Editors of *Cybersecurity*,

Please consider our manuscript, **"HiTrust-FedBot: Trust-Aware Hierarchical Federated Web Bot Detection with Group-Coverage-Constrained Filtering,"** for publication as a Research article in *Cybersecurity*.

This submission addresses adversarially robust federated bot detection under heterogeneous edge conditions. The paper makes three main contributions:

1. It introduces a grouped trust-aware federated defense that treats semantic group coverage as a first-class security constraint rather than as an incidental implementation detail.
2. It shows through multi-scenario experiments that the primary practical value of trust-aware filtering is conservative security control: reducing poisoned participation at near-neutral accuracy cost rather than claiming universal F1 gains.
3. It isolates the principal failure mode of the static group-floor rule and demonstrates a lightweight conditional trust-mass hardening that substantially repairs this edge case on the topology-aware mainline.

The manuscript is likely to be of interest to *Cybersecurity* readers because it sits at the intersection of federated cyber-defense, poisoning-robust learning, trustworthy edge intelligence, and graph-based security analytics. The work combines method design, adversarial evaluation, communication-efficiency analysis, and reproducibility-oriented artifact packaging.

We have tried to keep the claims appropriately conservative. In particular, the paper does not argue that trust-aware filtering universally improves F1. Instead, the strongest supported claim is that it reduces poisoned participation while usually preserving near-neutral detection performance, with explicit analysis of the remaining boundary cases.
